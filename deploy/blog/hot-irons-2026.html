3 irons: pros and cons

Srixon

ZXiR

From $1,099 (7 pcs)

Srixon's first true game-improvement iron; one outlet's best all-rounder of 2026.

✓ PROS
  • Remarkably soft feel for a game-improvement iron
  • Lots of forgiveness and great looks
  • Holds ball speed and distance on mishits
✗ CONS
  • Slightly large look at address
  • The HL model may launch too high for some

Best for: Mid-high handicaps who want easy irons with feel

Check price at 2nd Swing →
TaylorMade

Qi Max

From $1,099.99 (7 pcs)

A game-improvement iron designed to look more like a players iron.

✓ PROS
  • Excellent ball speed through the set
  • Design reduces long irons leaking right
  • Slight draw bias helps slicers
✗ CONS
  • Little performance gain over the 2024 Qi
  • Busier look than before, per some testers

Best for: Golfers without the previous Qi who want a clean, easy iron

Check price at 2nd Swing →
Mizuno

JPX 925 Hot Metal Pro

About $150/club

The better-player Hot Metal: thinnest topline, least offset.

✓ PROS
  • Explosive speed and high launch
  • Great looks
  • Fair price
✗ CONS
  • Occasional jumpers (fliers)

Best for: Low to mid handicaps wanting distance and looks
